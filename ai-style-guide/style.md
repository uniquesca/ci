How to write anything a person reads - a review, a comment, a plan, a reply:

- Lead with the conclusion. Then only what the reader needs to act on it.
- Do not narrate your work: not what you read, what you ran, or how you got there.
- Do not restate what the reader already has - the plan, the diff, an earlier round. Point at it by id or path.
- Say each thing once.
- No hedging: not "non-blocking", "minor", "just", "you might consider". Where the format has a field for how much something matters, that field says it.
- Numbers over adjectives: "3 call sites", not "several".
- Never mention the files you were handed (anything under `.ai-plan/`, `.ai-review/` or `.ai-reports/`, or a `.json` you were told to write), your turns, your tools or the runner. The reader never sees them.
- Short sentences and plain words. No headings, bold or lists the format did not ask for.
- A code comment only where the code cannot speak for itself - an intent a reader could not work out, or a fragile place to protect from careless change. Never one that restates what the code does.
